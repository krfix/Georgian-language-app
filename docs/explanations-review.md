# Explanations review: English and Polish (KART #8, 19/11/26)

This covers every explanation the learner reads in the course: the module intros, the "why" cards
shown before a lesson, the letter sounds and tips, the grammar notes, the feedback after an answer
and the notes on word cards. The full list, English next to Polish, is in the appendix at the end.
The phrasebook's notes are not included here; they can be reviewed the same way next.

Nothing has been changed in the app yet. Each finding gives the current text and a suggested
replacement. Findings marked **(check)** are ones where I am fairly sure but a native speaker should
confirm before we rely on them.

**Overall:** the explanations are in good shape. The Georgian facts are almost all right, and the
Polish versions are often better than the English: they compare with Polish in useful ways (cukier →
cukrem, pies → psy, podoba mi się, boli mnie głowa, wy-/po- for finished actions). I found
**9 real mistakes**, **10 places that can be clearer or shorter**, and **3 things for the Polish
side only**, the biggest being that the Polish explanations speak to men only.

## A. Mistakes (should be fixed)

**[M1] Instrumental -ით: "after a vowel just -თი" is wrong** (why card v9, EN and PL)
The example itself, მანქანა, ends in a vowel and still takes -ით (მანქანით). The rule: words in -ი or
-ა drop it and add -ით (მანქანა → მანქანით, ყავა → ყავით); words in -ო or -უ keep it and add -თი
(მეტრო → მეტროთი).
- EN now: "Add -ით for "by": მანქანით, by car; after a vowel just -თი: მეტროთი."
- EN new: "-ით means "by": მანქანით, by car. Words ending in -ო add -თი: მეტროთი, by metro."
- PL new: "Końcówka -ით to nasz narzędnik: მანქანით, samochodem. Słowa na -ო dostają -თი: მეტროთი, metrem."

**[M2] "Three teens change": it is four, and they are not really irregular** (grammar note v6c)
The note says three change (13, 17, 19), but the table below it shows 18 changing too. There is also a
simpler explanation that makes 13, 17 and 19 regular: the თ melts into the next sound.
თ + ს → ც (ცამეტი), თ + შ → ჩ (ჩვიდმეტი), თ + ც → ც (ცხრამეტი). Only 18 is special (რვ → ვრ).
- EN new: "Every teen is თ + the number + მეტი, "more": თ-ოთხ-მეტი, fourteen. Before ს, შ and ც the
  თ melts into the sound: ცამეტი (13), ჩვიდმეტი (17), ცხრამეტი (19). In 18, რვ turns to ვრ: თვრამეტი."
- PL: the same change (keep the nice "jede-naście" comparison).
- Word notes for ცამეტი, ჩვიდმეტი, ცხრამეტი: say "თ + სამ: თს melts into ც" etc., instead of
  "changed" / "with no თ in front".

**[M3] Food words and -ი: the examples contradict the point** (why card v7, EN and PL)
ჩაი is a bad example: its ი stays (ჩაის, not ჩას). And the example sentence ყავას ვსვამ uses ყავა,
which doesn't end in -ი at all. Also, -ი is not a food thing: almost every noun ending in a consonant
gets -ი in its plain form.
- EN new: "Most nouns end in -ი in their plain form: პური, ყველი. When the word does a job in the
  sentence, the -ი gives way to another ending: ყველს ვჭამ, I eat cheese."
- PL new: "Większość rzeczowników kończy się w mianowniku na -ი: პური, ყველი. Gdy słowo pełni inną
  rolę w zdaniu, -ი ustępuje innej końcówce, jak u nas „ser → jem ser”: ყველს ვჭამ."

**[M4] Handy patterns: შეიძლება has no "me" inside** (grammar note g9, EN and PL)
"Like მინდა, these have 'me' inside the verb" covers all three, but შეიძლება means "it is possible";
only მჭირდება and მომწონს have მ-.
- EN new: "მჭირდება and მომწონს work like მინდა, with მ- for "me". შეიძლება is "it is possible":
  on its own it asks "may I?"."
- PL new: "მჭირდება i მომწონს działają jak მინდა, z მ- jako „mi”. მომწონს to dokładnie „podoba mi
  się”. შეიძლება znaczy „można”: samo w sobie pyta „czy mogę?”."

**[M5] Number + noun in Polish: "w mianowniku" is wrong** (grammar note v6c, PL only)
The noun stays singular, but its case follows its job: ორ ყავას ვსვამ, შვიდ საათზე (the note's own
example has -ზე).
- PL now: "Po liczebniku rzeczownik zostaje w mianowniku liczby pojedynczej"
- PL new: "Po liczebniku rzeczownik zostaje w liczbie pojedynczej"

**[M6] "30, 50, 70 and 90 are all 'twenty and ten'"** (grammar note v6c, PL only)
50 is "two twenties and ten". New: "…wszystkie to „dwudziestki i dziesięć”: 50 to dwie dwudziestki
i dziesięć."

**[M7] Rolled r or tapped r?** (letter რ, EN and PL)
The sound line says "rolled r" and the tip says "a short tap"; Polish says "krótkie, drżące". Georgian
რ is usually a single tap. EN sound: "tapped r, as in Spanish pero". PL tip: "Krótkie r, jak polskie,
z jednym uderzeniem języka."

**[M8] "Family words don't change for my and your"** (why card v16, EN only)
It doesn't say anything ("my brother" doesn't change in English either). The Polish version has the
real point: ჩემი is the same for a brother and a sister.
- EN new: "და means both "sister" and "and". ჩემი, my, never changes: ჩემი ძმა, my brother;
  ჩემი და, my sister."

**[M9] "You'll hear მოუხვიეთ in every taxi"** (why card v11, EN and PL)
In a taxi it's the passenger who says "turn left". New: "…the polite "turn": you'll say it in every
taxi." PL: "…usłyszysz go od przechodniów i sam powiesz w taksówce."

Smaller facts:
- **v14, lari:** "ლარი is the money, თეთრი the coins": there are lari coins too. New: "თეთრი is the
  small change: 100 tetri make a lari. თეთრი also means "white"." (PL: "…თეთრი znaczy też „biały”".)
- **e2, order:** "name the thing and the amount: ერთი კილო ვაშლი": the amount comes first. New: "say
  the amount, then the thing".
- **ჩაი word note, PL:** "jak polskie czaj": czaj is Russian, not Polish. New: "Jak rosyjskie „czaj”".

## B. Clearer or shorter

**[C1] Tight sounds compared with "p in spin"** (letters პ, ტ, EN)
English p in spin is unaspirated but not "tight": nothing closes in the throat. It is a fine first
step, but say what makes it different. New პ tip: "Like the p in spin, plus a little pop from the
throat." The same for ტ. The Polish tips already say this well ("z zatrzymanym oddechem").

**[C2] ყ "with a little click"** (letter ყ, EN and PL)
"Click" is the name of a different kind of sound (as in tsk-tsk). New: "Further back than კ, with
the same tight pop." PL: "…z tym samym ściśniętym „pyknięciem”."

**[C3] ძ "dz as in adze"** (EN): few people know "adze". New: "dz as in adds".

**[C4] Contrast note: "Five sounds come in pairs"** (a9, EN and PL)
Reads as if five sounds make pairs; it is five pairs. New: "Five sounds have two versions: tight,
with no air (კ პ ტ წ ჭ), and with a puff of air, like English (ქ ფ თ ც ჩ). They make different
words: კარი is a door, ქარი the wind." The Polish note is twice as long; it can lose one of its
two tips (keep the hand in front of the mouth, drop the candle).

**[C5] Saying no: the English note doesn't explain anything** (g6)
"Put the little word right before the verb" doesn't say what არ, ვერ and ნუ are. The Polish note does.
New EN: "Georgian has three "nots", all right before the verb: არ, not; ვერ, can't (ვერ გავიგე, I
couldn't catch it); ნუ, don't, when asking someone (ნუ ღელავ, don't worry). არა alone is "no"."

**[C6] Present tense: add the two things the Polish note has** (g3, EN)
"შენ წერ" has no mark for "you", and the pronoun is usually dropped. New EN: "The start and end of
the verb show who: ვ- for I and we, -ს for he or she, -თ for plural, -ენ for they, nothing for you.
The pronoun is often left out: ვწერ alone is "I write"."

**[C7] "From where": -იდან and -დან** (why card v4)
The card says საიდან is სად + -იდან, then says "the same -დან". Make it one rule: "-იდან after a
consonant, -დან after a vowel: საიდან, თბილისიდან, კინოდან."

**[C8] Exclamation with რა** (why card e8)
"რა + a word in -ა" is confusing: the -ა is the short "is" from g2. New: "რა + the short -ა, is,
makes an exclamation: რა ამინდია! What weather!"

**[C9] Numbers 0–10 card: "No changing endings to learn"** (v5)
Numbers themselves do take endings (ორს, ორის, შვიდ საათზე). New: "After a number the noun stays
singular: ორი ჩაი, "two tea". No plural to worry about."

**[C10] -ივ "means towards"** (v11) **(check)**
I can't confirm -ივ as a "towards" ending; it is an old ending that makes adverbs. Safer: "მარჯვნივ
and მარცხნივ, right and left, come from მარჯვენა and მარცხენა."

## C. Polish only

**[P1] The Polish explanations speak to men only.**
"jakbyś zdmuchiwał", "Wybierz, co byś powiedział", "nie dałem rady zrozumieć", "Co zrobiłeś",
"sam buduj zdania". Many learners are women. Easy fix: write explanations in forms with no gender
(the present and commands have none): "jak przy zdmuchiwaniu świeczki", "Wybierz, co chcesz
powiedzieć", "nie udało mi się zrozumieć", "Przeszłość", "buduj zdania samodzielnie". Example
sentences ("Byłem na targu") can keep one form, as long as typing answers accept both (the word
list already accepts byłem/byłam; worth checking the sentences too).

**[P2] Two comparisons that don't work**
- **Short -ა (g2):** "jak mówimy „gdzieś” zamiast „gdzie jesteś”". "Gdzieś" means "somewhere"; the
  comparison confuses more than it helps. New: "Podobnie jak angielskie it is → it's: ექიმი არის →
  ექიმია." (or no comparison at all)
- **Polite you (g12):** "jak dawniej na polskiej wsi". For many Poles "wy" as a polite form sounds
  like communist-era officialese ("obywatelu, wy…"). New: "jak francuskie vous".
- Smaller: "მაქვს, „mi jest”" (g4) isn't Polish; just leave out the literal for მაქვს.
  კვირა word note: "Po rosyjsku неделя, czyli nasza „niedziela”, to właśnie tydzień."

**[P3] One choice for "want coffee"**
g4 asks "Jak powiedzieć „chcę kawy”?", g5 shows "Chcesz kawę?". Both are Polish; using one ("kawy")
looks more careful.

## D. Ideas worth adding (short, useful in Georgia)

1. **Capitals on signs** (alphabet intro): "no capitals" is true, but shop signs and headings often
   use a taller style of the same letters (Mtavruli). One sentence saves confusion in the street.
2. **ჰ for Poles** (letter ჰ, PL): most Poles say h like ch. Tip: "Sam lekki wydech, bez tarcia:
   lżej niż polskie ch."
3. **ის also means "that"** (g1): "ეს is this, ის that, he, she or it."
4. **Plural things, singular verb** (g11) **(check)**: the lesson's own sentence სახლები დიდია uses
   "is", not "are", which a learner can't explain from the notes. One line: "Things in the plural
   often take "is": სახლები დიდია, the houses are big."
5. **დედაჩემი** (v16): in real life Georgians usually say "my mum" as one word, დედაჩემი,
   მამაჩემი. Needs glossary entries.
6. **Yes/no questions** (g5) **(check)**: "raise your voice at the end" is a simplification; Georgian
   questions often rise and then fall on the very last syllable. "Copy the melody in the audio" is
   safe and short.

## 2. "Why" cards (before a lesson starts)

**v1 · Hello and goodbye** · *Hello means victory* / *„Cześć” znaczy „zwycięstwo”*  
EN: გამარჯობა comes from გამარჯვება, victory: you wish someone victory. The reply, გაგიმარჯოს, wishes it back. Greetings for the time of day end in მშვიდობისა, "of peace".  
PL: გამარჯობა pochodzi od გამარჯვება, zwycięstwo: życzysz komuś zwycięstwa. Odpowiedź, გაგიმარჯოს, życzy go z powrotem. Powitania o porze dnia kończą się na მშვიდობისა, „pokoju”, jak staropolskie „pokój z tobą”.

**v2 · Please and thank you** · *Two kinds of thanks* / *Dwa rodzaje „dziękuję”*  
EN: მადლობა is everyday thanks. გმადლობთ is polite: the გ- means "you" and the -თ makes it polite. You'll meet this -თ everywhere.  
PL: მადლობა to zwykłe „dzięki”. გმადლობთ jest grzeczne: გ- to „ci/panu”, a -თ dodaje grzeczności, jak nasze „dziękuję panu”. To -თ spotkasz wszędzie.

**v3 · Who are you?** · *Called, not named* / *„Nazywa mi się”*  
EN: Georgian says "to me it is called": მე ნინო მქვია. The მ- inside the verb is "me". To ask: რა გქვია? with გ- for "you".  
PL: Gruzin mówi „nazywa mi się”: მე ნინო მქვია. მ- w środku czasownika to nasze „mi”. Pytanie: რა გქვია?, z გ- jako „ci”: „jak ci na imię?”, prawie jak po polsku!

**v4 · Where are you from?** · *From where* / *Skąd*  
EN: საიდან means "from where": სად (where) + -იდან (from). The same -დან turns any place into "from": თბილისიდან, from Tbilisi.  
PL: საიდან to „skąd”: სად (gdzie) + -იდან (z, od). Ta sama końcówka -დან robi z każdego miejsca „z”: თბილისიდან, z Tbilisi. Gdzie my mamy przyimek z przodu, Gruzin dokleja końcówkę.

**v5 · Zero to ten** · *Counting to ten* / *Liczymy do dziesięciu*  
EN: Georgian numbers are short and regular. After a number the noun stays singular: ორი ჩაი, "two tea". No changing endings to learn.  
PL: Gruzińskie liczebniki są krótkie i regularne. Po liczebniku rzeczownik zostaje w liczbie pojedynczej: ორი ჩაი, „dwie herbata”. Żadnych polskich „dwie herbaty, pięć herbat”.

**v6 · Eleven to nineteen** · *Ten and more* / *Dziesięć i więcej*  
EN: The teens are built from ten and the number: თ (from ათი, ten) + ოთხ (four) + მეტი (more) makes თოთხმეტი, fourteen. Look for the pattern in each one.  
PL: „Nastki” buduje się z dziesięciu i liczby: თ (od ათი, dziesięć) + ოთხ (cztery) + მეტი (więcej) daje თოთხმეტი, czternaście. Jak nasze „cztery-naście”, tylko w odwrotnej kolejności.

**v6b · Twenty to a hundred** · *Counting in twenties* / *Liczenie dwudziestkami*  
EN: Above twenty Georgian counts in twenties: ორმოცი is "two twenties". The next lesson shows how to build every number from these.  
PL: Powyżej dwudziestu Gruzini liczą dwudziestkami: ორმოცი to „dwie dwudziestki”. Następna lekcja pokazuje, jak z tego zbudować każdą liczbę.

**v7 · At the table** · *Food words* / *Słowa o jedzeniu*  
EN: Many Georgian food words end in -ი: პური, ყველი, ჩაი. That -ი is the plain form; it changes when the word does a job in the sentence: ყავას ვსვამ, I drink coffee.  
PL: Wiele gruzińskich słów kończy się na -ი: პური, ყველი, ჩაი. To -ი to mianownik; zmienia się, gdy słowo pełni inną rolę w zdaniu, jak u nas „kawa → piję kawę”: ყავას ვსვამ.

**v8 · Georgian kitchen** · *Georgian kitchen* / *Kuchnia gruzińska*  
EN: ხაჭაპური and ხინკალი are names you'll see on every menu. Words for things you eat often come in the singular: ხილი is fruit, ბოსტნეული vegetables.  
PL: ხაჭაპური i ხინკალი zobaczysz w każdym menu. Jedzenie często występuje w liczbie pojedynczej: ხილი to owoce, ბოსტნეული warzywa, jak nasze „owoc, warzywo” w zbiorczym znaczeniu.

**v9 · Getting around** · *Getting around* / *Poruszanie się po mieście*  
EN: Most transport words are international: ავტობუსი, მეტრო, ტაქსი. Add -ით for "by": მანქანით, by car; after a vowel just -თი: მეტროთი.  
PL: Większość słów o transporcie jest międzynarodowa: ავტობუსი, მეტრო, ტაქსი. Końcówka -ით to nasz narzędnik: მანქანით, samochodem; po samogłosce -თი: მეტროთი, metrem.

**v10 · Travelling far** · *Travelling far* / *Dalekie podróże*  
EN: Long words are often built from parts: თვითმფრინავი is "self-flying". აეროპორტი and სადგური take -ში for "to": აეროპორტში, to the airport.  
PL: Długie słowa składa się z części: თვითმფრინავი to „sam-latający”, zupełnie jak polski samolot. აეროპორტი i სადგური dostają -ში dla „do”: აეროპორტში, na lotnisko.

**v11 · Left and right** · *Directions* / *Kierunki*  
EN: მარჯვნივ and მარცხნივ end in -ივ, which means "towards". The polite command is მოუხვიეთ, turn: you'll hear it in every taxi.  
PL: მარჯვნივ i მარცხნივ kończą się na -ივ, „w stronę”, jak nasze „w prawo”. Grzeczny rozkaz to მოუხვიეთ, „proszę skręcić”: usłyszysz go w każdej taksówce.

**v12 · Near and far** · *Near and far* / *Blisko i daleko*  
EN: ახლოს and შორს are said with არის, is: ახლოს არის, it's near. Put the place first: ბაზარი ახლოს არის?  
PL: ახლოს i შორს mówi się z არის, „jest”: ახლოს არის, jest blisko. Miejsce stoi na początku: ბაზარი ახლოს არის?, „targ blisko jest?”.

**v13 · Shops and markets** · *Shops and prices* / *Sklepy i ceny*  
EN: რა ღირს? literally "what is it worth?": the everyday "how much?". ღია and დაკეტილი, open and closed, are on every shop door.  
PL: რა ღირს? to dosłownie „ile to warte?”, codzienne „ile kosztuje?”. ღია i დაკეტილი, otwarte i zamknięte, są na drzwiach każdego sklepu.

**v14 · Paying** · *Paying* / *Płacenie*  
EN: ლარი is the money, თეთრი the coins (100 tetri in a lari). By card is ბარათით: ბარათი + -ით, "with the card".  
PL: ლარი to waluta, თეთრი to grosze (100 tetri w jednym lari). Kartą to ბარათით: ბარათი + -ით, nasz narzędnik.

**v15 · Parents and children** · *Mother and father* / *Matka i ojciec*  
EN: Careful: დედა is mother and მამა is father, the opposite of what you might expect. შვილი is someone's child, ბავშვი a child in general.  
PL: Uwaga: დედა to matka, a მამა to ojciec, odwrotnie niż podpowiada polskie ucho! შვილი to czyjeś dziecko (syn, córka), ბავშვი to dziecko w ogóle.

**v16 · The whole family** · *The whole family* / *Cała rodzina*  
EN: და means both "sister" and "and": the sentence makes it clear. Family words don't change for my and your: ჩემი ძმა, my brother.  
PL: და to i „siostra”, i „i”: zdanie wyjaśnia, o co chodzi. ჩემი (mój) nie zmienia się dla rodzaju: ჩემი ძმა, mój brat; ჩემი და, moja siostra.

**v17 · At home** · *At home* / *W domu*  
EN: Places take -ში for "in": სახლში, at home; ოთახში, in the room. Things on top take -ზე: მაგიდაზე, on the table.  
PL: Miejsca dostają -ში dla „w”: სახლში, w domu; ოთახში, w pokoju. Na wierzchu: -ზე, მაგიდაზე, na stole. Przyimek, tylko na końcu.

**v18 · Things around you** · *Things around you* / *Rzeczy wokół ciebie*  
EN: To say a thing is somewhere, add -ა (short for არის, is) to the place: წიგნი მაგიდაზეა, the book is on the table.  
PL: Żeby powiedzieć, że coś gdzieś jest, dodaj -ა (skrót od არის, „jest”) do miejsca: წიგნი მაგიდაზეა, książka jest na stole.

**v19 · Today and tomorrow** · *Today and tomorrow* / *Dziś i jutro*  
EN: Time words need no little words before them: დღეს, today; ხვალ, tomorrow. დილით and საღამოს are "in the morning" and "in the evening".  
PL: Słowa o czasie nie potrzebują przyimków: დღეს, dziś; ხვალ, jutro. დილით i საღამოს to „rano” i „wieczorem”: końcówki robią z nich „kiedy?”, jak nasze „wieczor-em”.

**v20 · Days and weeks** · *Days of the week* / *Dni tygodnia*  
EN: From Monday to Thursday the days count from Saturday: ორშაბათი is "two after Saturday". კვირა is both Sunday and week.  
PL: Od poniedziałku do czwartku dni liczy się od soboty: ორშაბათი to „dwa po sobocie”. Podobnie nasze „wtorek, czwartek, piątek” to „drugi, czwarty, piąty”. კვირა to i niedziela, i tydzień.

**e1 · Ordering food** · *Asking politely* / *Grzeczne prośby*  
EN: In cafés Georgians add თუ შეიძლება, "if it's possible", after what they ask for: მენიუ, თუ შეიძლება. It's the everyday please.  
PL: W kawiarni Gruzini dodają თუ შეიძლება, „jeśli można”, po tym, o co proszą: მენიუ, თუ შეიძლება. To codzienne „poproszę”.

**e2 · Buying groceries** · *At the shop* / *W sklepie*  
EN: To buy, name the thing and the amount: ერთი კილო ვაშლი. To check the price: რა ღირს ეს? Bargaining is normal at a bazaar.  
PL: Kupując, podaj ilość i rzecz: ერთი კილო ვაშლი, „kilo jabłko”, bez dopełniacza. Cena: რა ღირს ეს? Na bazarze targowanie się jest normalne.

**e3 · Taking a taxi** · *In a taxi* / *W taksówce*  
EN: Drivers say თქვენ to you, the polite you, with -თ on the verb: სად მიდიხართ? Answer with the place and -ში: აეროპორტში.  
PL: Kierowcy mówią do ciebie თქვენ, grzecznie, z -თ na czasowniku: სად მიდიხართ?, „dokąd pan jedzie?”. Odpowiadasz miejscem z -ში: აეროპორტში, na lotnisko.

**e4 · Asking the way** · *Asking the way* / *Pytanie o drogę*  
EN: Commands to strangers end in -თ: წადით, go; მოუხვიეთ, turn. If you don't follow, ვერ გავიგე: "I couldn't catch it".  
PL: Polecenia do obcych kończą się na -თ: წადით, proszę iść; მოუხვიეთ, proszę skręcić. Jeśli nie nadążasz: ვერ გავიგე, „nie dałem rady zrozumieć”.

**e5 · Meeting the neighbours** · *Meeting neighbours* / *Poznajemy sąsiadów*  
EN: With neighbours you don't know yet, use the polite forms: როგორ ხართ? With friends: როგორ ხარ?  
PL: Z sąsiadami, których jeszcze nie znasz, mów grzecznie: როგორ ხართ?, „jak się pan ma?”. Ze znajomymi: როგორ ხარ?

**e6 · At the doctor's** · *Saying what hurts* / *Co boli*  
EN: Georgian says "to me hurts": თავი მტკივა, my head hurts. The body part is the subject.  
PL: Gruzin mówi dokładnie jak Polak: თავი მტკივა, „boli mnie głowa”. Część ciała jest podmiotem, a მ- to „mnie”.

**e7 · At work** · *At work* / *W pracy*  
EN: ვმუშაობ is "I work": ვ- for I. Where you work takes -ში: ოფისში ვმუშაობ.  
PL: ვმუშაობ to „pracuję”: ვ- oznacza „ja”. Miejsce pracy dostaje -ში: ოფისში ვმუშაობ, pracuję w biurze.

**e8 · Small talk** · *Small talk* / *Pogawędka*  
EN: Weather is said with one word: ცხელა, it's hot; ცივა, it's cold. რა + a word in -ა makes an exclamation: რა ამინდია! What weather!  
PL: Pogodę mówi się jednym słowem: ცხელა, gorąco; ცივა, zimno, jak nasze „zimno!”. რა + słowo na -ა to wykrzyknik: რა ამინდია! Co za pogoda!

**c1 · At the café** · *Your first conversation* / *Twoja pierwsza rozmowa*  
EN: Choose what you would say. The waiter is polite (რა გნებავთ? what would you like?); you answer with თუ შეიძლება. Tap any word to see why it looks that way.  
PL: Wybierz, co byś powiedział. Kelner mówi grzecznie (რა გნებავთ?, czego pan sobie życzy?), ty odpowiadasz z თუ შეიძლება. Stuknij dowolne słowo, żeby zobaczyć, dlaczego tak wygląda.

**c2 · Meeting someone new** · *Introducing yourself* / *Przedstawiamy się*  
EN: Giorgi is your age, so you both say შენ and use friendly forms: რა გქვია? not რა გქვიათ?  
PL: Giorgi jest w twoim wieku, więc mówicie sobie na ty (შენ): რა გქვია?, a nie grzeczne რა გქვიათ?

**c3 · At the market** · *At the market* / *Na targu*  
EN: Prices are numbers + ლარი, with ლარი singular: თხუთმეტი ლარი. Haggling is part of it: ეს ძალიან ძვირია.  
PL: Ceny to liczba + ლარი, zawsze w liczbie pojedynczej: თხუთმეტი ლარი, „piętnaście lari”. Targowanie się należy do zabawy: ეს ძალიან ძვირია.

**c4 · In a taxi** · *Taking a taxi* / *Jazda taksówką*  
EN: To the airport is აეროპორტში: -ში means "into" as well as "in". Agree the price before you set off.  
PL: Na lotnisko to აეროპორტში: -ში znaczy i „w”, i „do”. Gruziński nie odróżnia „gdzie?” od „dokąd?”. Cenę ustal przed odjazdem.

**c5 · Asking the way** · *Asking the way* / *Pytanie o drogę*  
EN: Listen for მარჯვნივ, მარცხნივ, პირდაპირ. If it's too fast: გაიმეორეთ, თუ შეიძლება.  
PL: Nasłuchuj მარჯვნივ, მარცხნივ, პირდაპირ (w prawo, w lewo, prosto). Za szybko? გაიმეორეთ, თუ შეიძლება, „proszę powtórzyć”.

**c6 · Plans for tomorrow** · *Making plans* / *Planujemy*  
EN: Friends use the informal forms. ვჭამოთ is "let's eat": the -თ here means "we".  
PL: Znajomi mówią sobie na ty. ვჭამოთ to „zjedzmy”: -თ oznacza tu „my”, jak nasze „-my” w „zjedzmy”.

## 4. Contrast lesson note (a9)

EN: Five sounds come in pairs: one tight, with no air (კ პ ტ წ ჭ), and one with a puff of air, like English (ქ ფ თ ც ჩ). They make different words: კარი is a door, ქარი is the wind.  
PL: Pięć dźwięków występuje w parach: jeden ściśnięty, bez wydechu (კ პ ტ წ ჭ), drugi z wyraźnym dmuchnięciem powietrza (ქ ფ თ ც ჩ). Polskie p, t, k są bez wydechu, więc bliżej im do ściśniętych; przy tych z wydechem dmuchnij, jakbyś zdmuchiwał świeczkę. To zmienia znaczenie: კარი to drzwi, a ქარი to wiatr. Przyłóż dłoń do ust: przy ქ poczujesz powietrze, przy კ prawie nic.

## 5. Grammar notes (inside lessons)

### v6c · How numbers are built

**Eleven to nineteen: ten and more** / **Od 11 do 19: dziesięć i więcej**  
EN: Every teen is თ + the number + მეტი, "more": თ-ოთხ-მეტი is "ten, four more", fourteen. Three of them change a little to be easier to say: ცამეტი (13), ჩვიდმეტი (17) and ცხრამეტი (19).  
PL: Każda „nastka” to თ (skrót od ათი, dziesięć) + liczba + მეტი, „więcej”: თ-ოთხ-მეტი to „dziesięć, cztery więcej”, czyli czternaście. Polak zna ten pomysł: „jede-naście” to dawne „jeden na dziesięć”. Tylko kolejność jest odwrotna: Gruzin mówi najpierw dziesięć, a potem jeden. Trzy liczby trochę się zmieniają, żeby łatwiej się je wymawiało: ცამეტი (13), ჩვიდმეტი (17) i ცხრამეტი (19).

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| თერთმეტი | 11: თ + ერთ + მეტი | 11: თ + ერთ + მეტი |
| თორმეტი | 12: თ + ორ + მეტი | 12: თ + ორ + მეტი |
| ცამეტი | 13: from სამი, changed | 13: od სამი, zmienione |
| თოთხმეტი | 14: თ + ოთხ + მეტი | 14: თ + ოთხ + მეტი |
| თხუთმეტი | 15: თ + ხუთ + მეტი | 15: თ + ხუთ + მეტი |
| თექვსმეტი | 16: თ + ექვს + მეტი | 16: თ + ექვს + მეტი |
| ჩვიდმეტი | 17: from შვიდი, changed | 17: od შვიდი, zmienione |
| თვრამეტი | 18: თ + რვა + მეტი, რვ turns to ვრ | 18: თ + რვა + მეტი, რვ zmienia się w ვრ |
| ცხრამეტი | 19: ცხრა + მეტი | 19: ცხრა + მეტი |

**Counting in twenties** / **Liczenie dwudziestkami**  
EN: From twenty up, Georgian counts in twenties, a little like French quatre-vingts (four twenties, 80). ორმოცი is "two twenties", სამოცი "three twenties", ოთხმოცი "four twenties".  
PL: Od dwudziestu w górę Gruzini liczą dwudziestkami, trochę jak Francuzi w quatre-vingts (cztery dwudziestki, 80). Wyobraź sobie, że po polsku 40 to „dwie dwudziestki”: ორმოცი to właśnie ორ (dwa) + მ + ოცი (dwadzieścia). Tak samo სამოცი, „trzy dwudziestki” (60), i ოთხმოცი, „cztery dwudziestki” (80).

|---|---|---|
| ოცი | 20 | 20 |
| ორმოცი | 40: two twenties | 40: dwie dwudziestki |
| სამოცი | 60: three twenties | 60: trzy dwudziestki |
| ოთხმოცი | 80: four twenties | 80: cztery dwudziestki |
| ასი | 100 | 100 |

**Twenty and…** / **Dwadzieścia i…**  
EN: To go past a twenty, drop its last ი, add და ("and"), then a number from 1 to 19. So 21 is "twenty and one", 30 is "twenty and ten" and 31 is "twenty and eleven".  
PL: Żeby wyjść poza dwudziestkę, odetnij jej końcowe ი, dodaj და („i”) i liczbę od 1 do 19. Dlatego 21 to „dwadzieścia i jeden”, 30 to „dwadzieścia i dziesięć”, a 31 to „dwadzieścia i jedenaście”. Nie ma osobnego słowa na 30, 50, 70 ani 90: wszystkie są „dwudziestka i dziesięć”.

|---|---|---|

| ოცდაორი | 22: twenty and two | 22: dwadzieścia i dwa |
| ოცდახუთი | 25: twenty and five | 25: dwadzieścia i pięć |
| ოცდაათი | 30: twenty and ten | 30: dwadzieścia i dziesięć |
| ოცდათერთმეტი | 31: twenty and eleven | 31: dwadzieścia i jedenaście |
| ოცდაცხრამეტი | 39: twenty and nineteen | 39: dwadzieścia i dziewiętnaście |
| ორმოცდაორი | 42: two twenties and two | 42: dwie dwudziestki i dwa |
| ორმოცდაათი | 50: two twenties and ten | 50: dwie dwudziestki i dziesięć |

**A number and a noun** / **Liczebnik i rzeczownik**  
EN: After a number the noun stays singular: ორი ყავა is "two coffee". Before a noun with an ending, the number drops its ი: შვიდ საათზე, "at seven o'clock".  
PL: Tu gruziński jest łatwiejszy od polskiego. Po liczebniku rzeczownik zostaje w mianowniku liczby pojedynczej: ორი ყავა to dosłownie „dwie kawa”, ხუთი ლარი „pięć lari”, bez naszych „dwie kawy, pięć kaw”. Przed rzeczownikiem z końcówką liczebnik gubi ი: შვიდ საათზე, „o siódmej”.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| ორი ყავა | two coffees | dwie kawy |
| სამი ბილეთი | three tickets | trzy bilety |
| ოცი ლარი | twenty lari | dwadzieścia lari |
| შვიდ საათზე | at seven o'clock | o siódmej |

### g1 · I, you, we

**Personal pronouns** / **Zaimki osobowe**  
EN: Georgian has six. There is no he or she: ის means he, she and it.  
PL: Jest ich sześć, jak po polsku, ale gruziński nie ma rodzajów: ის to i „on”, i „ona”, i „ono”. Z tego samego powodu czasownik nie zmienia się jak nasze „był/była”. თქვენ to „wy” do kilku osób, a także grzecznościowe „pan/pani” do jednej: Gruzin mówi do obcego „wy”, a czasownik dostaje końcówkę -თ.

| ის | he, she, it | on, ona, ono |
| ჩვენ | we | my |
| თქვენ | you (several people, or polite) | wy; pan, pani (grzecznie) |

### g2 · To be

**I am, you are** / **Jestem, jesteś**  
EN: The verb "to be" changes with the person. It usually comes at the end of the sentence.  
PL: Czasownik „być” odmienia się przez osoby, tak jak polskie jestem, jesteś, jest. Zwykle stoi na końcu zdania: მე სტუდენტი ვარ, dosłownie „ja student jestem”, trochę jak u Yody.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| მე ვარ | I am | jestem |
| შენ ხარ | you are | jesteś |
| ის არის | he / she is | on / ona jest |
| ჩვენ ვართ | we are | jesteśmy |
| თქვენ ხართ | you are (plural) | jesteście |
| ისინი არიან | they are | są |

**The short -ა** / **Krótkie -ა**  
EN: არის is often shortened to -ა at the end of the word before it.  
PL: არის („jest”) często skraca się do samego -ა doklejonego do poprzedniego słowa. Podobnie jak w mowie potocznej mówimy „gdzieś” zamiast „gdzie jesteś”: ექიმი არის → ექიმია.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| ნინო ექიმი არის | Nino is a doctor | Nino jest lekarką |
| ნინო ექიმია | Nino is a doctor (short) | Nino jest lekarką (krótko) |

### g3 · Doing things now

**The present tense** / **Czas teraźniejszy**  
EN: The start and end of a verb show who is doing it: ვ- for I and we, -ს for he or she, -თ for plural, -ენ for they.  
PL: Po polsku osobę pokazuje końcówka: piszę, piszesz, pisze. Gruziński używa i początku, i końca czasownika: ვ- z przodu to „ja” i „my”, -ს na końcu to „on/ona”, -თ to liczba mnoga, a -ენ to „oni”. ვ-წერ-თ to więc „my piszemy”: ვ- (ja/my) + -თ (mnoga). Zaimek często się pomija, jak u nas: ვწერ wystarczy za „piszę”.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| მე ვწერ | I write | piszę |
| შენ წერ | you write | piszesz |
| ის წერს | he / she writes | on / ona pisze |
| ჩვენ ვწერთ | we write | piszemy |
| თქვენ წერთ | you write (plural) | piszecie |
| ისინი წერენ | they write | piszą |

**Word order** / **Szyk zdania**  
EN: The verb usually comes last: მე ქართულს ვსწავლობ, "I Georgian learn". Other orders are fine too.  
PL: Czasownik zwykle stoi na końcu: მე ქართულს ვსწავლობ, „ja gruzińskiego się uczę”. Szyk jest jednak swobodny, jak po polsku, bo to końcówki mówią, kto co robi.

### g4 · Want, love, have

**Verbs that work the other way** / **Czasowniki „na odwrót”**  
EN: Some everyday verbs put "me" inside the verb with მ-, and "you" with გ-. The thing you want or have stays in its plain form.  
PL: Kilka codziennych czasowników działa jak polskie „podoba mi się” albo „chce mi się”: osoba siedzi w czasowniku jako „mi” albo „ci”. მ- to „mi/mnie”, გ- to „ci/tobie”. მინდა to dosłownie „mi się chce”, მიყვარს „mi jest miłe”, მაქვს „mi jest”. Rzecz, którą chcesz albo masz, zostaje w mianowniku: ყავა მინდა, „kawa mi się chce”.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| მინდა | I want | chcę |
| გინდა | you want | chcesz |
| მიყვარს | I love | kocham |
| გიყვარს | you love | kochasz |
| მაქვს | I have | mam |
| გაქვს | you have | masz |

**Regular verbs** / **Zwykłe czasowniki**  
EN: Others work like ვწერ.  
PL: Inne odmieniają się jak ვწერ, z ვ- dla „ja”.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| ვიცი | I know | wiem |
| ვლაპარაკობ | I speak | mówię |
| ვჭამ | I eat | jem |
| ვსვამ | I drink | piję |

### g5 · Asking questions

**Question words** / **Słowa pytające**  
EN: The question word usually comes just before the verb.  
PL: Słowo pytające stoi zwykle tuż przed czasownikiem: სად არის? „gdzie jest?”.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| რა? | what? | co? |
| ვინ? | who? | kto? |
| სად? | where? | gdzie? |
| როდის? | when? | kiedy? |
| რატომ? | why? | dlaczego? |
| როგორ? | how? | jak? |
| რამდენი? | how many? | ile? |

**Yes or no** / **Tak czy nie**  
EN: Keep the sentence as it is and raise your voice at the end.  
PL: Zostaw zdanie tak, jak jest, i podnieś głos na końcu, tak samo jak po polsku: „Chcesz kawę?”. Nie ma tu słówka „czy”.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|

### g6 · Saying no

**Not, can't, don't** / **Nie, nie mogę, nie rób**  
EN: Put the little word right before the verb.  
PL: Gruziński ma trzy „nie”, a każde stoi tuż przed czasownikiem, jak nasze „nie”. არ to zwykłe „nie”. ვერ to „nie mogę, nie dam rady” (ვერ გავიგე, „nie dałem rady zrozumieć”). ნუ to „nie rób tego” w prośbach (ნუ ღელავ, „nie martw się”). Samo არა to „nie” jako odpowiedź.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| არ ვიცი | I don't know | nie wiem |
| ვერ გავიგე | I couldn't understand | nie dałem rady zrozumieć |
| ნუ ღელავ | don't worry | nie martw się |
| არა | no (on its own) | nie (jako odpowiedź) |

### g7 · Word endings

**Cases** / **Przypadki**  
EN: A Georgian noun changes its ending to show its job in the sentence. Five to start with:  
PL: Dobra wiadomość: gruziński ma przypadki, tak jak polski, więc znasz już ten pomysł. Rzeczownik zmienia końcówkę zależnie od roli w zdaniu. Gruzińskich przypadków jest siedem, a końcówki są regularne, bez polskich wyjątków. Na początek pięć: mianownik (-ი), celownik-biernik (-ს), dopełniacz (-ის), narzędnik (-ით, dokładnie jak nasze „samochodem”) i okolicznik (-ად, „po gruzińsku”).

|---|---|---|

| მანქანით | by car (-ით) | samochodem (narzędnik: -ით) |

### g8 · In, on, from

**Endings for places** / **Końcówki miejsca**  
EN: Where English puts a little word before, Georgian adds an ending.  
PL: Gdzie polski stawia przyimek przed słowem („w domu”, „na stole”), gruziński dokleja końcówkę na końcu: სახლ-ში, „dom-w”. To jak przyimki, tylko przyklejone z tyłu.

### g9 · Handy patterns

**Three patterns for every day** / **Trzy schematy na co dzień**  
EN: Like მინდა, these have "me" inside the verb.  
PL: Tak jak მინდა, mają „mi” w środku czasownika. მომწონს działa dokładnie jak polskie „podoba mi się”: to rzecz się podoba, a ty jesteś „mi”. Podobnie მჭირდება, „potrzebne mi jest”.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| თუ შეიძლება | please (if it's possible) | proszę (jeśli można) |
| შეიძლება? | may I? is it OK? | można? czy mogę? |
| მჭირდება | I need | potrzebuję |
| მომწონს | I like (it pleases me) | podoba mi się |

### g10 · Things and people

**Two ways to have** / **Dwa rodzaje „mieć”**  
EN: Georgian has two verbs for "have": მაქვს for things, მყავს for people and animals. Both put "me" inside the verb with მ-, like მინდა, and "you" with გ-.  
PL: Gruziński ma dwa czasowniki „mieć”: მაქვს dla rzeczy i მყავს dla ludzi i zwierząt. Trochę jak gdyby po polsku inaczej się „miało” telefon, a inaczej brata. Oba mają „mi” w środku (მ-), jak მინდა, a „ci” to გ-.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| ფული მაქვს | I have money | mam pieniądze |
| მანქანა მაქვს | I have a car | mam samochód |
| ძმა მყავს | I have a brother | mam brata |
| ძაღლი მყავს | I have a dog | mam psa |
| გაქვს? | do you have (a thing)? | masz (rzecz)? |
| გყავს? | do you have (someone)? | masz (kogoś)? |

### g11 · More than one

**Plurals with -ები** / **Liczba mnoga z -ები**  
EN: Most nouns drop their final -ი and add -ები: სახლი, სახლები. Nouns ending in a vowel keep it: დედა, დედები. A few lose a vowel inside, as in მეგობრები.  
PL: Większość rzeczowników gubi końcowe -ი i dostaje -ები: სახლი, სახლები. Słowa na samogłoskę ją zachowują: დედა, დედები. Jedna końcówka dla wszystkich, bez polskich „-y, -i, -e, -owie”. Niektóre gubią samogłoskę w środku, jak მეგობრები (jak nasze „pies → psy”).

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| სახლი | house | dom |
| სახლები | houses | domy |
| წიგნი | book | książka |
| წიგნები | books | książki |
| მეგობარი | friend | przyjaciel |
| მეგობრები | friends: the second ა drops | przyjaciele: drugie ა znika |
| დედა | mother | matka |
| დედები | mothers | matki |

EN: After a number the noun stays singular: სამი წიგნი, "three book". The plural is only for "books" with no number.  
PL: Po liczebniku rzeczownik zostaje w liczbie pojedynczej: სამი წიგნი, „trzy książka”. Liczba mnoga jest tylko bez liczebnika.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| სამი წიგნი | three books | trzy książki |

### g12 · Polite you

**The polite -თ** / **Grzecznościowe -თ**  
EN: Georgian has a polite "you": თქვენ, which is also "you" for several people. The verb gets -თ at the end: გაქვს becomes გაქვთ, იცი becomes იცით. Use it with strangers, in shops, with older people and officials.  
PL: Polak mówi do obcego „pan/pani” w trzeciej osobie. Gruzin mówi „wy”: თქვენ, jak dawniej na polskiej wsi. Czasownik dostaje -თ na końcu: გაქვს → გაქვთ, იცი → იცით. Używaj tego z obcymi, w sklepach, ze starszymi i w urzędach.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| გაქვს? | do you have? (to a friend) | masz? (do znajomego) |
| გაქვთ? | do you have? (polite) | ma pan/pani? (grzecznie) |
| იცი? | do you know? (to a friend) | wiesz? (do znajomego) |
| იცით? | do you know? (polite) | wie pan/pani? (grzecznie) |
| როგორ ხარ? | how are you? (to a friend) | jak się masz? (do znajomego) |
| როგორ ხართ? | how are you? (polite) | jak się pan/pani ma? (grzecznie) |

**Polite requests** / **Grzeczne prośby**  
EN: Requests end in -თ too, and თუ შეიძლება makes them softer still: აქ გამიჩერეთ, თუ შეიძლება.  
PL: Prośby też kończą się na -თ, a თუ შეიძლება dodatkowo je łagodzi, jak nasze „proszę”: აქ გამიჩერეთ, თუ შეიძლება.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| მოიცადე | wait (to a friend) | poczekaj (do znajomego) |
| მოიცადეთ | wait (polite) | proszę poczekać |
| დაჯექი | sit down (to a friend) | usiądź (do znajomego) |
| დაჯექით | sit down (polite) | proszę usiąść |

### g13 · Yesterday

**Things you did** / **Co zrobiłeś**  
EN: The past has its own forms: learn these six as words first. Each has ვ- for "I", at the start or after a little prefix: წა-ვ-ედი, და-ვ-ლიე.  
PL: Czas przeszły ma własne formy: naucz się tych sześciu jak słówek. Każda ma ვ- dla „ja”, na początku albo po małym przedrostku: წა-ვ-ედი, და-ვ-ლიე. Te przedrostki działają jak polskie „wy-, po-, z-”: robią z czasownika dokonany (piłem → wypiłem).

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| ვიყავი | I was | byłem, byłam |
| წავედი | I went | poszedłem, poszłam |
| ვნახე | I saw | zobaczyłem, zobaczyłam |
| ვჭამე | I ate | zjadłem, zjadłam |
| დავლიე | I drank | wypiłem, wypiłam |
| ვიყიდე | I bought | kupiłem, kupiłam |

**The object changes** / **Dopełnienie się zmienia**  
EN: In the present the thing you eat takes -ს: ხინკალს ვჭამ. In the past it goes back to its plain form: ხინკალი ვჭამე.  
PL: W czasie teraźniejszym to, co jesz, dostaje -ს: ხინკალს ვჭამ. W przeszłym wraca do mianownika: ხინკალი ვჭამე. Po polsku tak się nie dzieje, więc po prostu to zapamiętaj: przeszłość, forma podstawowa.

| Georgian | EN | PL |
|---|---|---|
| ხინკალს ვჭამ | I'm eating khinkali | jem chinkali |
| ხინკალი ვჭამე | I ate khinkali | zjadłem chinkali |
| ყავას ვსვამ | I'm drinking coffee | piję kawę |
| ყავა დავლიე | I drank coffee | wypiłem kawę |

| შენ (you) | "You" for one person you know well. | „Ty” do jednej osoby, którą znasz, jak polskie „ty”. |
| ენა (language) | — | Jak po polsku: język w ustach i język, którym mówimy. |
| ორი (two) | After a number the noun stays singular: ორი ჩაი, "two tea". | Po liczebniku rzeczownik zostaje w liczbie pojedynczej: ორი ჩაი, dosłownie „dwie herbata”. Prościej niż po polsku: żadnych „pięć kotów”. |
| თერთმეტი (eleven) | თ + ერთ + მეტი: "ten, one more". All the teens are built this way. | თ + ერთ + მეტი: „dziesięć, jeden więcej”. Jak nasze „jede-naście”, czyli „jeden na dziesięć”. |
| თორმეტი (twelve) | თ + ორ + მეტი: ten, two more. | თ + ორ + მეტი: dziesięć, dwa więcej. |
| ცამეტი (thirteen) | From სამი, three: ცა + მეტი. One of the three teens that change a little. | Od სამი, trzy: ცა + მეტი. Jedna z trzech „nastek”, które trochę się zmieniają. |
| თოთხმეტი (fourteen) | თ + ოთხ + მეტი: ten, four more. | თ + ოთხ + მეტი: dziesięć, cztery więcej. |
| თხუთმეტი (fifteen) | თ + ხუთ + მეტი: ten, five more. | თ + ხუთ + მეტი: dziesięć, pięć więcej. |
| თექვსმეტი (sixteen) | თ + ექვს + მეტი: ten, six more. | თ + ექვს + მეტი: dziesięć, sześć więcej. |
| ჩვიდმეტი (seventeen) | From შვიდი, seven: შ becomes ჩ, ჩვიდ + მეტი. | Od შვიდი, siedem: შ zmienia się w ჩ, ჩვიდ + მეტი. |
| თვრამეტი (eighteen) | თ + რვა + მეტი, with რ and ვ swapping places: თვრამეტი. | თ + რვა + მეტი, a რ i ვ zamieniają się miejscami: თვრამეტი. |
| ცხრამეტი (nineteen) | ცხრა + მეტი, with no თ in front. | ცხრა + მეტი, bez თ na początku. |
| ოცი (twenty) | Georgian counts in twenties: 30 is "twenty and ten", 40 is "two twenties". | Gruziński liczy dwudziestkami: 30 to „dwadzieścia i dziesięć”, 40 to „dwie dwudziestki”. |
| ოცდაერთი (twenty-one) | ოც-და-ერთი: twenty and one. ოცი drops its ი and adds და, "and". | ოც-და-ერთი: dwadzieścia i jeden. ოცი gubi ი i dostaje და, „i”. |
| ოცდაათი (thirty) | ოც-და-ათი: twenty and ten. | ოც-და-ათი: dwadzieścia i dziesięć. |
| ორმოცი (forty) | ორ-მ-ოცი: two twenties. | ორ-მ-ოცი: dwie dwudziestki. |
| ორმოცდაათი (fifty) | ორმოც-და-ათი: two twenties and ten. | ორმოც-და-ათი: dwie dwudziestki i dziesięć. |
| ჩაი (tea) | — | Prawie jak polskie „czaj”, które też przyszło ze Wschodu. |
| ხაჭაპური (khachapuri) | Bread baked with cheese, Georgia's best-known dish. | Chleb pieczony z serem, najsłynniejsze gruzińskie danie. |
| ხინკალი (khinkali) | Twisted dumplings filled with meat and broth, eaten by hand. | Skręcane pierogi z mięsem i rosołem w środku, je się je rękami. Kuzyn naszych pierogów. |
| თვითმფრინავი (aeroplane) | — | Dosłownie „sam-latający”, zupełnie jak polski „samolot”! |
| ლარი (lari) | Georgia's money. One lari is 100 tetri (თეთრი). | Gruzińska waluta. Jedno lari to 100 tetri (თეთრი). |
| დედა (mother) | Careful: დედა is mother and მამა is father. | Uwaga: დედა to matka, a მამა to ojciec, odwrotnie niż podpowiada ucho! |
| და (sister) | და also means "and". | და znaczy też „i”. |
| შვილი (child (son or daughter)) | — | Czyjeś dziecko: syn albo córka. Dziecko w ogóle to ბავშვი. |
| კვირა (week; Sunday) | კვირა means both "week" and "Sunday". | კვირა to i „tydzień”, i „niedziela”. Podobnie u Słowian: rosyjska „niedziela” (неделя) to tydzień. |
| ორშაბათი (Monday) | Days from Monday: ორშაბათი, სამშაბათი, ოთხშაბათი, ხუთშაბათი — "two, three, four, five after Saturday". | Dni od poniedziałku: ორშაბათი, სამშაბათი, ოთხშაბათი, ხუთშაბათი, czyli „dwa, trzy, cztery, pięć po sobocie”. |
| შაბათი (Saturday) | — | Jak polska „sobota”: oba słowa pochodzą od hebrajskiego „szabat”. |
